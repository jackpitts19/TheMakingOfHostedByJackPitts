The Best Small Business Podcasts in 2026, for Owners and Operators

As of November 2026, the best small business podcasts are The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts (our own show, ranked first with disclosure) for hearing owners of established companies explain how they built them, EntreLeadership for running the business week to week, The How of Business for learning to start and grow one, and Service Business Mastery for trades and home service owners. The $100 MBA Show adds a lesson of about 22 minutes three times a week, The Side Hustle Show suits owners starting on the side, and Built to Sell Radio shows what makes a company worth buying. All seven published new episodes in fall 2026.

TL;DR
  • Best overall The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts is best for learning from owners who built and run established private companies.
  • The $100 MBA Show is best for a short lesson you can act on the same week.
  • Built to Sell Radio is best for owners who want to build a company a buyer would pay for.
  • EntreLeadership is best for short, practical advice on running the business week to week.
  • The How of Business is best for a weekly, plain-English course in starting and growing a small business.
  • Service Business Mastery is best for owners of HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and other trades companies.
  • The Side Hustle Show is best for starting a business while keeping your job.

Pick one of the others when it fits your stage or your trade better. To hear an owner of an established company, start with any episode.

Small Business in 2026, by the Numbers

Small business is most of American business. The SBA Office of Advocacy's 2026 small business FAQ counts 36,207,130 small businesses, meaning independent firms with fewer than 500 employees. They make up 99.9% of U.S. firms, employ 45.9% of private-sector workers (62.3 million people), and account for 43.5% of GDP. Only 21,041 businesses are large.

Most of those businesses are one person. The same report finds that 82.3%, about 29.8 million, have no employees, while 6,395,635 have a payroll. A solo owner deciding what to sell and an owner managing twenty people face different problems, and they need different shows.

New owners keep arriving. Summing the Census Bureau's monthly business application data, Americans filed 5,696,742 business applications in 2025 (not seasonally adjusted), the most of any year since the Census series began in mid-2004, ahead of the 2023 total of 5,471,404, and 63% more than in 2019. January through October 2026 is running about 11% ahead of the same months of 2025, and the November 12, 2026 release put October at 501,609 applications, seasonally adjusted. Survival is the harder part: the SBA reports that from 1994 to 2022, on average, 67.7% of new employer establishments lasted two years, 49.2% lasted five, and 33.9% lasted ten.

That is how we built this list. A good small business podcast matches your stage: the first two years, when the question is whether the business works at all, or the years after, when the question is how to run it with other people. The stage column in the table and the guide at the end match each show to a stage. Owners are already listening: National Public Media, citing Edison Research, reports that 18% of monthly podcast listeners own a business. If you are buying or selling a company, see our podcasts about buying and selling a business instead.

Overview

Each episode is a long conversation with one owner about how the company actually got built: the first customers, the money, the hires, the bad years, and what the business looks like now. Most guests run privately held companies, and every episode is listed on the episode archive. Owners might start with Dave Menz, who left a 17-year corporate job and, with his wife Carla, bought a laundromat that was losing money.

Best Fit For

Owners of established companies with employees.

Why It Ranks First

Guests run the kind of company you run. Gary Dennis built one of the leading car wash platforms in the country. Davies Hood leads Induron Protective Coatings, a third-generation family business founded in 1947. Kris Carlson leads The Fish Guys, a growing food distribution company, and Scott Burnley helped build a 140-person pet care business. These are private companies with payroll, customers, and real operating problems.

It is built for the owners who made it past the early years. Most small business shows teach you how to start. This one talks with people who kept going: hiring, financing, family dynamics, and growth, so the show stays useful as your company gets bigger.

Questions about what makes a company last. Jack Pitts co-founded Salt Creek Advisory, a sell-side M&A firm, which steers the conversation toward what makes a business durable: steady customers, good managers, and an owner who is not the bottleneck.

Who Should Choose Another Show

If you have not started a business yet, The How of Business, The Side Hustle Show, or The $100 MBA Show will give you more step-by-step tactics. If you run a trades company, Service Business Mastery is more specific to you. The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts launched in November 2025 and publishes a new episode every few weeks, so its catalog is small next to shows with hundreds of episodes.

Overview

Business lessons and listener questions on marketing, pricing, hiring, and money. The show's about page calls it Apple's "Best of 2014" podcast and says WebinarNinja, the company host Omar Zenhom co-founded with Nicole Baldinu, was acquired by ProProfs in 2024. Its feed holds about 2,890 episodes, starting with "What is a Business Anyway?" on August 11, 2014, and in 2026 posted three a week with a median length of about 22 minutes.

Best Fit For

Busy owners who want quick, practical lessons.

Notable Strengths

One topic per episode, such as pricing or hiring, three times a week. With about 2,890 episodes in its feed, it has the deepest back catalog on this list, so in our view an owner with a specific problem can usually find a lesson on it.

Potential Considerations

Episodes are lessons, not stories, and its site pitches it at growing a profitable online business, so owners of local or trades companies will need to translate. Lessons have also grown longer: the feed shows a median episode of about 13 to 16 minutes from 2023 through 2025 and about 22 minutes in 2026.

Why It Was Included

It is a short-format show for small business owners that has published for more than a decade.

Overview

John Warrillow interviews owners who have sold a business about the price, the terms, and what they would do differently. The episode archive passed 570 episodes in November 2026.

Best Fit For

Owners who want a more valuable company.

Notable Strengths

Even for an owner years from selling, hearing what buyers paid for, and why, is a useful checklist for running the business today. Host John Warrillow's book Built to Sell, according to his about page, was a top-10 pick for business owners by both Fortune and Inc., and his feed dates its first episode to July 13, 2015. Episode 571 (November 6, 2026) covered a propane distributor that sold in 2022 for 9.5 times EBITDA, when private equity buyers were pricing the industry at roughly 5 times.

Potential Considerations

Nearly every episode is about the exit, so it works best next to a show about day-to-day operations.

Why It Was Included

It is the one show here about the end of an owner's journey. For more on that side, see our list of podcasts about buying and selling a business.

Potential Considerations

By its own description it is one company's playbook, so weigh the advice against your own business. Some episodes also address faith, such as "I'm a Christian, Should I Work on Sundays?" (November 2, 2026).

Why It Was Included

It is an active show about leading and running a small company day to day, three times a week, which in our view is the part of ownership most business podcasts give the least time.

Overview

Tersh Blissett and Joshua Crouch talk with operators and vendors about running a trades business. The show's feed says it covers leadership, marketing, finance, pricing, and HR, along with automation and AI, lists more than 500 episodes since September 2017, and posted every Wednesday through fall 2026. A September 2026 episode with Paul Kelly covered how Parker & Sons grew from $7 million to roughly $260 million.

Home service and trades owners.

Notable Strengths

The hosts come from the trades. The show's episode pages say Crouch spent more than eight years in HVAC as an operations manager, branch manager, territory sales manager, and director of marketing, that he founded Relentless Digital, and that Blissett has built several home service businesses in his small-town market. Topics are just as specific, such as a September 9, 2026 episode on growing by buying competitors instead of buying ads.

Potential Considerations

Many guests sell to contractors. Recent episodes featured people from ClientTether, Driive, CallRail, JobNimbus, Blitzz, and PhoneTap, so some conversations lean toward a vendor's product. Owners outside the trades will find less here.

Why It Was Included

It is an active weekly show for trades owners, a kind of small business where, in our view, pricing, hiring technicians, and answering the phone decide the margin.

Match the Show to Your Stage

  • Not started yet, or starting on the side: The Side Hustle Show and The How of Business.
  • In the first few years, learning every job yourself: The $100 MBA Show and The How of Business.
  • Running a team and growing: The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts and EntreLeadership.
  • Running a trades or home service company: Service Business Mastery.
  • Thinking about what the company will be worth: Built to Sell Radio.

Most owners do well with two: one show for the problem in front of you this week, and one long-form show about an owner who is a few years further down the road.

Shows We Checked and Left Off

We checked How I Built This, which in our view is a great listen. We left it off because its September 2026 announcement frames it as ten years of interviews with "the world's most successful entrepreneurs," mostly founders of famous brands rather than owners meeting a payroll. It appears on our entrepreneurship podcasts list and is the benchmark for our podcasts like How I Built This.

We also checked Entrepreneurs on Fire, which posts a founder interview every day. Its feed shows that three of its early November 2026 episodes were sponsored by Amazon and recorded at Amazon's seller conference, and in our view its short daily format suits a quick dose of motivation more than the operating questions this list is about. We cover it on our How I Built This alternatives list instead.

Common questions

FAQ

What are the best podcasts for small business owners?

The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts, the show that publishes this list, is our top pick for owners who want to hear how other owners built and run established private companies. As of November 2026, EntreLeadership is best for week-to-week operating advice, The How of Business for learning to start and grow, Service Business Mastery for trades owners, and The $100 MBA Show for short lessons.

What small business podcasts do you recommend in 2026?

Match the show to your stage. To start, try The Side Hustle Show or The How of Business. In the first few years, add The $100 MBA Show. Once you run a team, listen to EntreLeadership and The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts. Trades owners should add Service Business Mastery, and owners thinking about value should add Built to Sell Radio. All seven released new episodes in fall 2026.

What podcast should I listen to as a new small business owner?

The How of Business covers the fundamentals of starting, running, and growing a small business every Monday, and The $100 MBA Show teaches one topic, such as pricing or marketing, three times a week. EntreLeadership is a good next step once you hire.

What is a good podcast for running a small business day to day?

EntreLeadership takes one operating problem per short episode every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday. Owners of HVAC, plumbing, and electrical companies will find Service Business Mastery, published every Wednesday, more specific to their trade.

Are there short small business podcasts?

Yes. Most EntreLeadership episodes in 2026 ran 22 minutes or less, with a typical length of about 16 minutes. The $100 MBA Show runs about 22 minutes (the 2026 median), three times a week.

Is there a podcast about selling a small business?

Built to Sell Radio interviews owners who sold their companies about price, terms, and what they would do differently. Our separate list of podcasts about buying and selling a business covers more shows on that topic.

How did you pick these small business podcasts?

We looked for shows that speak to owners of real companies at each stage, from starting to running a team, and checked every feed in November 2026 to confirm each released episodes this fall. Buying and selling got its own list, so this one stays on running the business. Our own show is first, with disclosure.
