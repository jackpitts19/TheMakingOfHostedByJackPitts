The Best Startup Podcasts in 2026: Our Pick and 7 More, Compared

The best startup podcasts in 2026 are The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts (our own show, ranked first with disclosure) for how a startup becomes a lasting company, The Twenty Minute VC for venture fundraising, the Y Combinator Startup Podcast for early-stage advice, and Lenny's Podcast for product-market fit. In our view, The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts is the best choice if you want founders explaining the whole arc, from first funding to running a company for decades.

TL;DR
  • Best overall The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts is best for hearing how founders turn a startup into a company that lasts.
  • Acquired is best for strategy lessons drawn from famous companies' full histories.
  • Invest Like the Best with Patrick O'Shaughnessy is best for long conversations on capital, markets, and company building.
  • Lenny's Podcast is best for tactical advice on product, growth, and product-market fit.
  • Masters of Scale is best for growth lessons once you have something to scale.
  • This Week in Startups is best for keeping up with early-stage startup and angel news.
  • The Twenty Minute VC (20VC) is best for learning how venture capitalists think before you raise.
  • Y Combinator Startup Podcast is best for early-stage advice straight from an accelerator's partners.

Pick one of the others when you need help with this quarter's problem, such as a raise or product-market fit. To hear founders on the years after, start with any episode.

Podcast Listening in 2026

Podcast listening is at a record high. The Infinite Dial 2026, released by Edison Research at SSRS with support from SiriusXM Media, found that 58% of Americans age 12 and older, about 167 million people, consumed a podcast in the last month, an all-time high. Forty-five percent, about 130 million people, did so in the last week, and 57% have both listened to and watched a podcast.

Business owners are well represented in that audience. National Public Media, citing Edison Research's Infinite Dial 2025, reports that 18% of monthly podcast listeners own a business, that 47% earn $75,000 or more a year, and that 51% have a college degree.

With that many shows competing for the same hour, the useful question is not which show is biggest. It is which one is built for a listener like you.

What the 2026 Startup Market Means for Your Listening

The startup money of 2026 is large and concentrated. Crunchbase's third-quarter funding report found that global venture funding reached $159 billion across close to 6,000 startups in Q3 2026, down about 25% from Q2 but up about 53% from a year earlier, with a record 27 companies raising rounds of $1 billion or more. Crunchbase also reported in June that so far in 2026, nearly 88% of AI-related startup funding, or $319 billion, went to U.S.-headquartered companies.

Company building is changing too. In a September 2026 episode, Y Combinator's partners said that nearly one in five YC companies now has a solo founder and that startups are reaching meaningful revenue faster than ever.

That is why one show is rarely enough. Funding-focused shows explain how capital moves, product shows explain how to find customers, and founder interviews explain what the years after the first round actually look like. Most founders, in our view, need one of each.

Overview

Each episode is a long conversation with one owner about how the company actually got built: the first customers, the money, the hires, the bad years, and what the business looks like now. Most guests run privately held companies, and every episode is listed on the episode archive. Founders in the thick of it might start with Jesse Choe, who raised $1 million at 19 and went through Y Combinator.

Best Fit For

Founders who want the whole arc, from first round to a business that lasts.

Why It Ranks First

Both ends of the startup story. The show talks with founders in the middle of it, such as a Y Combinator founder who raised $1 million at 19 and an AI infrastructure startup that raised $5.1 million, and with owners who built companies that have lasted decades. Hearing both shows you where the startup years actually lead.

Not every startup is a software company. In our view, most startup shows assume venture capital and a tech product. Here you also hear from the owners of franchises, distributors, manufacturers, and service companies.

A view from the far end of the startup story. Because Jack Pitts advises owners when they sell, the questions keep returning to what makes a company last and hold its value after the first rounds: margins, customers, hiring, and debt.

Who Should Choose Another Show

It is a young show, launched in November 2025, so the catalog is small next to shows with hundreds of episodes, and it does not cover startup news. If you are raising venture money this quarter, pick The Twenty Minute VC or This Week in Startups. If you are hunting for product-market fit, pick Lenny's Podcast or the Y Combinator Startup Podcast.

Why It Was Included

Founder and investor interviews, often about an hour, several a week
Owner
Independent (the 20VC brand)

Overview

Harry Stebbings interviews venture capitalists and founders about raising, investing, and markets. The show's feed description says past investor guests include Sequoia's Doug Leone and Benchmark's Bill Gurley, and past founder guests include Spotify's Daniel Ek and LinkedIn's Reid Hoffman. The same feed lists more than 1,500 episodes and added new ones about three times a week through October and early November 2026.

Best Fit For

Founders preparing to raise venture money.

Notable Strengths

In our view it is a clear window into how investors actually decide. If you are about to raise, hearing partners explain what they look for, and what makes them pass, is direct preparation for the meeting.

Potential Considerations

Despite the name, episodes often run about an hour. The lens is the investor's, so it says less about running a company day to day, and it matters most if you plan to raise venture money at all.

Why It Was Included

It is a long-running, frequently published interview show about venture funding, one of the biggest decisions many startup founders face.

How to Choose the Right Startup Podcast

  • You want to build a company that lasts: The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts, then Acquired for how famous companies did it.
  • You are about to raise venture money: The Twenty Minute VC and Invest Like the Best with Patrick O'Shaughnessy.
  • You are a first-time founder at the idea or seed stage: the Y Combinator Startup Podcast.
  • You are still searching for product-market fit: Lenny's Podcast.
  • You want weekly startup news: This Week in Startups.
  • You are scaling past the early stage: Masters of Scale.

Pair a tactical show with a founder story show. Advice tells you what to do this week, and long founder conversations tell you what the next ten years can look like.

Common questions

FAQ

What is the best startup podcast in 2026?

The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts, the show that publishes this list, is our top pick for founders who want to hear how a startup becomes a lasting company. Among the others, in our view, The Twenty Minute VC stands out for fundraising, the Y Combinator Startup Podcast for early-stage advice, and Lenny's Podcast for finding product-market fit.

What podcast should I listen to before raising venture capital?

The Twenty Minute VC interviews venture capitalists and founders about how investors decide, and Invest Like the Best with Patrick O'Shaughnessy goes deep on investors and markets. This Week in Startups covers early-stage funding news several times a week.

Is the Lightcone podcast still active?

Yes, but in a different feed. Based on the two feeds as of November 2026, the standalone Lightcone feed stopped updating in December 2025, and Lightcone episodes now publish in Y Combinator's own Startup Podcast feed, which released dozens of episodes in 2026.

Who hosts Masters of Scale now?

The Masters of Scale feed describes a set of hosts, with Reid Hoffman billed as founding host. Jeff Berman is credited as host on many recent episodes, including a conversation with Walmart CEO John Furner recorded live at the October 2026 Masters of Scale Summit.

Are there startup podcasts for founders who are not building tech companies?

Yes. The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts interviews founders of franchises, distributors, manufacturers, and service companies as well as venture-backed startups. Acquired also covers companies well outside software, such as Home Depot.

How did you choose the shows on this list?

We matched shows to the questions founders face in order: what to build, how to find product-market fit, how to raise, and how to scale. Every show published in 2026, and the figures we cite come from each show's feed or site. Our own show is first, with disclosure.
