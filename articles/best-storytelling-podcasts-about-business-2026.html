The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts is the best choice if you want the story of a real, privately held company from the person who built it.

Podcast Listening in 2026

Podcast listening is at a record high. The Infinite Dial 2026, released by Edison Research at SSRS with support from SiriusXM Media, found that 58% of Americans age 12 and older, about 167 million people, consumed a podcast in the last month, an all-time high. Forty-five percent, about 130 million people, did so in the last week, and 57% have both listened to and watched a podcast.

Business owners are well represented in that audience. National Public Media, citing Edison Research's Infinite Dial 2025, reports that 18% of monthly podcast listeners own a business, that 47% earn $75,000 or more a year, and that 51% have a college degree.

With that many shows competing for the same hour, the useful question is not which show is biggest. It is which one is built for a listener like you.

Why Business Storytelling Podcasts Matter in 2026

In our view, stories are how most people remember business lessons. A margin percentage fades, but the owner who started out selling ice or the four veterans who started a bourbon brand stays with you. And the podcast audience those stories reach keeps growing. National Public Media's summary of the 2026 Infinite Dial calls 58% monthly and 45% weekly podcast listening among Americans record levels. Edison Research found that listeners 55 and older are driving the growth: monthly online audio listening in that group rose from 52% in 2024 to 70% in 2026.

The business of narrative shows is changing too. In August 2025, Engadget reported that Amazon was splitting up Wondery and that its more narrative-focused podcasts, including Business Wars, would merge with Amazon's Audible team. In our view that is a sign that heavily produced storytelling is moving toward premium, subscription-backed homes.

Business storytelling comes in three main forms. Narrated shows retell a company's story from research. Reported shows follow a journalist through one story. Interview shows let the founder tell it firsthand. We think the best listening mix includes at least two of the three.

Why It Ranks First

The story comes from the person who lived it. Many business storytelling shows have a narrator or reporter retell what happened. Here the owner tells it: the first customers, the money, the hires, and the bad years.

Stories you have not heard before. Famous companies get told and retold. This show's guests run design firms, bourbon brands, pet care companies, and family manufacturers, so the stories are new and closer to the businesses most people work in.

Stories with a second act. Most business stories stop at the breakthrough. Because Jack Pitts advises owners on selling their companies, he keeps going: what the business became, who runs it now, and where it goes next.

Who Should Choose Another Show

It is an interview show, not a scripted production, and it launched in November 2025, so the catalog is small. If you want sound design and dramatized scenes, pick Business Wars. How to Choose the Right Storytelling Show

  • You want the story from the person who lived it: The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts for owners of private companies, How I Built This for well-known brands.
  • You want a famous company's full history: Acquired.
  • You want drama and sound design: Business Wars.
  • You have half an hour and want a reported story: WSJ's The Journal for business news, Planet Money for the economics behind it.
  • You want the numbers behind the story: Business Breakdowns.
  • You like big ideas about incentives and markets: Freakonomics Radio.

Pair a firsthand founder show with one narrated or reported show. Hearing a story from the person who lived it and from someone looking in from outside gives a truer picture than either alone.

Common questions

FAQ

What is the best storytelling podcast about business?

The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts, the show that publishes this list, is our top pick for listeners who want owners of privately held companies telling their own stories. Among the others, Acquired tells the full history of famous companies, Business Wars dramatizes corporate rivalries, and Planet Money explains the economy through short reported stories.

What are the best business history podcasts?

Acquired tells the full, researched history of one famous company per episode, and Business Wars dramatizes long rivalries between companies. Business Breakdowns takes one company apart with an investor or operator who has studied it.

Is Business Wars still made by Wondery?

Business Wars was created by Wondery. Engadget reported in August 2025 that Amazon was splitting up Wondery and moving its narrative shows, including Business Wars, to Audible, and the show's Apple Podcasts listing now carries it as an Audible Original. David Brown hosts it.

What is a good short business story podcast?

WSJ's The Journal tells the story behind one business news item in about 18 to 30 minutes each weekday, and Planet Money runs about 20 to 35 minutes and explains the economy through stories about businesses.

Are there narrative business podcasts like a documentary?

Yes. Business Wars is a scripted, dramatized series told in multi-episode arcs, Acquired tells long researched company histories, and Freakonomics Radio produces reported documentary episodes, often in multi-part series.

How did you choose the shows on this list?

We wanted one strong example of each way to tell a business story: firsthand interview, edited interview, narrated history, dramatized series, and reported news, each released in 2026.
