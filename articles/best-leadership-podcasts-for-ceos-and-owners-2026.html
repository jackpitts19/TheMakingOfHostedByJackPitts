The Best Leadership Podcasts for CEOs and Business Owners in 2026

The best leadership podcasts for CEOs and business owners in 2026 are The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts (our own show, ranked first with disclosure) for how owners lead private companies, HBR IdeaCast for research-backed management, Coaching for Leaders for skills like delegation, and The Knowledge Project for decision-making. The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts is the best choice for owners who want to hear how other owners handle hiring, growth, and succession.

Podcast Listening in 2026

Podcast listening is at a record high. The Infinite Dial 2026, released by Edison Research at SSRS with support from SiriusXM Media, found that 58% of Americans age 12 and older, about 167 million people, consumed a podcast in the last month, an all-time high. Forty-five percent, about 130 million people, did so in the last week, and 57% have both listened to and watched a podcast.

Business owners are well represented in that audience. National Public Media, citing Edison Research's Infinite Dial 2025, reports that 18% of monthly podcast listeners own a business, that 47% earn $75,000 or more a year, and that 51% have a college degree.

With that many shows competing for the same hour, the useful question is not which show is biggest. It is which one is built for a listener like you.

Why Leadership Skills Matter More in 2026

The case for spending an hour a week on leadership is in the numbers. Gallup's State of the Global Workplace: 2026 Report puts global employee engagement at 20% in 2025, the lowest since 2020. Manager engagement has fallen nine points since 2022, to 22% in 2025, including a five-point drop from 2024.

Gallup estimates that low engagement cost the world economy about $10 trillion in lost productivity in 2025, roughly 9% of global GDP. In a small or mid-sized company, the owner is often the manager whose engagement sets the tone for everyone else.

That is why the shows below are judged on whether they help a leader act differently on Monday morning, not on how famous their guests are.

Overview

Each episode is a long conversation with one owner about how the company actually got built: the first customers, the money, the hires, the bad years, and what the business looks like now. Most guests run privately held companies, and every episode is listed on the episode archive. For leadership across generations, try Davies Hood, who leads a third-generation family manufacturer.

Best Fit For

Owners and CEOs of privately held companies.

Why It Ranks First

Leadership as owners actually practice it. Many leadership shows feature professors, authors, and executives of very large companies. This show talks with the people who sign the payroll at manufacturers, distributors, franchises, and family businesses, and asks how they hired, delegated, handled partners, and kept people through hard years.

Leadership questions a buyer would ask. Because Jack Pitts advises owners when they sell, he asks whether the business runs without the owner, who the next leaders are, and how succession will work.

Whole stories, not tips. Episodes run about an hour, which leaves room to hear how a leader's role changed from founder doing everything to owner running a team.

Who Should Choose Another Show

It is a young show, launched in November 2025, and it is a conversation show, not a management course. If you want research and frameworks, pick HBR IdeaCast or The Knowledge Project. If you want a specific skill each week, pick Coaching for Leaders.

Overview

Dave Stachowiak interviews authors and practitioners about one management skill per episode. The show's feed says it has been independently produced weekly since 2011 and reports 50 million downloads and more than 300,000 followers. Stachowiak draws on a decade leading a global leadership academy and more than 15 years of leadership at Dale Carnegie. Episode 808, dated 9 November 2026 in the feed, covered finding the person to replace you.

Best Fit For

Managers and owners stepping into bigger roles.

Notable Strengths

Episode titles name one skill, such as repairing a stakeholder relationship or turning conflict into progress. In our view the conversations stay on how to do it rather than on the guest's biography, which makes it one of the more practical leadership feeds for managers moving into bigger roles.

Potential Considerations

It is a skills show, not a story show, so you will not hear how a company was built. Most recent guests are authors and consultants rather than owners, so you will need to apply the lessons to your own business.

Why It Was Included

It has published weekly since 2011 and regularly covers topics owners face, such as delegation and succession.

How to Choose the Right Leadership Podcast

  • You own or run a private company: The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts, then Coaching for Leaders for specific skills.
  • You want the research behind good management: HBR IdeaCast.
  • You want to make better decisions: The Knowledge Project.
  • You are scaling and need to let go of work: WorkLife with Molly Graham and Masters of Scale.
  • You want a weekly habit of leadership conversations: The Learning Leader Show with Ryan Hawk.
  • You want broader personal growth: The Diary of a CEO.

Pair one show about how real owners lead with one show about a skill or idea. Stories show you what good leadership looks like; skills shows help you practice it.

Common questions

FAQ

What is the best leadership podcast for CEOs in 2026?

The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts, the show that publishes this list, is our top pick for owners and CEOs who want to hear how leaders of privately held companies actually run them. Among the others, HBR IdeaCast covers research-backed management, The Knowledge Project focuses on decision-making, and Coaching for Leaders teaches one practical skill per episode.

What is a good leadership podcast for small business owners?

The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts interviews founders and owners, mostly of privately held, operating companies, about hiring, delegation, and succession. Coaching for Leaders covers practical management skills such as delegation and finding the person to replace you.

Who hosts HBR IdeaCast now?

Harvard Business Review lists Alison Beard, an executive editor, and Adi Ignatius, its editor at large, as the hosts of HBR IdeaCast.

Is Adam Grant still the host of WorkLife?

No. Molly Graham announced on her Substack on April 14, 2026 that she was taking over as host of WorkLife, the TED show Adam Grant created, and TED's show page now carries her host bio.

Which leadership podcasts have short episodes?

HBR IdeaCast episodes run about 25 to 35 minutes, and Coaching for Leaders interviews run about 35 to 40 minutes. The Knowledge Project, The Learning Leader Show, and The Diary of a CEO are long-form.

How did you choose the shows on this list?

We asked whether each show helps a leader act differently on Monday morning, not how famous its guests are, and kept only shows that published in 2026. The list mixes skills, research, decisions, and owner stories. Our own show is first, with disclosure.
