The Best Entrepreneurship Podcasts in 2026: Our Pick and 7 More, Compared

As of November 2026, the best entrepreneurship podcasts are The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts (our own show, ranked first with disclosure) for owners of privately held companies, How I Built This for founder origin stories, Acquired for company histories, Founders for lessons from biographies, and My First Million for business ideas. If you are about to start a business, pair My First Million, which helps you pick an idea, with a show where an owner explains how a real company was built. The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts is, in our view, the best choice for the second half of that pair. All eight shows released new episodes in 2026, and every fact below is cited.

TL;DR
  • Best overall The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts is best for hearing owners of privately held, operating companies tell the whole story.
  • Acquired is best for deep, researched histories of famous companies.
  • The Diary of a CEO is best for long, personal conversations that go beyond business.
  • Founders is best for lessons from biographies of history's great builders.
  • How I Built This is best for hearing how famous companies got off the ground.
  • Masters of Scale is best for short, current interviews with CEOs about growing a company.
  • My First Million is best for business ideas and the energy to chase one.
  • The Tim Ferriss Show is best for the habits and tools of top performers.

Starting from zero? Pair My First Million for ideas with one show where founders tell the whole story. To hear an owner who began in a spare bedroom, start with Melissa Banko.

Starting a Business in 2026: What the Numbers Say

More Americans are trying to start companies than at any point in recent years. By our calculation from Census Bureau data, 5,696,742 business applications were filed in the U.S. in 2025, the most of any year since the Census series began in mid-2004, ahead of the 2023 total of 5,471,404, and 63% more than the 3,498,990 filed in 2019. From January through October 2026, 5,360,944 were filed, up 10.9% from the same months of 2025.

Few of those applications become companies with a payroll. The Census Bureau's November 12, 2026 release counted 501,609 seasonally adjusted applications in October 2026, but projected only 27,867 new employer businesses from them within four quarters, roughly one in 18 by our math. The Kauffman Foundation found that 360 of every 100,000 adults became new entrepreneurs in an average month of 2025, that 83.3% of them were what Kauffman calls opportunity entrepreneurs, and that its startup early survival rate, the share of new firms still operating after their first year, was 77.9%.

People who follow business are listening, too. In a Sounds Profitable and Signal Hill Insights survey of 5,071 U.S. adults, fielded in June 2024 and published that December, 15% of monthly podcast consumers had heard a business podcast in the past month. Of those business listeners, 78% listened weekly, averaging 7.2 hours a week against a weekly average of 6, and 86% said they were likely to try a podcast recommended by someone in their social circle, most often (67%) because they like to learn new things. Pew Research Center found in 2025 that 54% of U.S. adults had listened to a podcast in the past year.

What that means for choosing a show: most new companies will be small, privately held, and funded by their owners, and the hard part is the stretch between filing the paperwork and making payroll. In our view the best entrepreneurship podcast for you is one whose guests faced that stretch at your scale. Chart rankings, in our view, mostly reflect fame, so pair a famous-founder show with one about ordinary owners. Our separate guide, How to Choose an Entrepreneurship Podcast, explains how to test any show in fifteen minutes.

Overview

Each episode is a long conversation with one owner about how the company actually got built: the first customers, the money, the hires, the bad years, and what the business looks like now. Most guests run privately held companies, and every episode is listed on the episode archive. If you are just starting out, begin with Melissa Banko, who started Banko Design in a spare bedroom in 2014, then Dave Moorman, who built the IT services firm DynaSis over 26 years, sold it to Novatech, helped complete more than 10 acquisitions there, and went through a second exit.

Best Fit For

Owners, and people about to become one.

Why It Ranks First

Its guests look like the companies most founders will actually build. The SBA's Office of Advocacy counts 36,207,130 small businesses in the U.S., 99.9% of all firms. Most entrepreneurship shows interview founders of famous venture-backed or consumer brands. This one spends its hour with owners of manufacturers, distributors, franchises, service companies, and family businesses.

Questions from someone who sees how the story ends. Jack Pitts co-founded a sell-side M&A firm, so he asks founders about the early choices that decide what a company is eventually worth: the first hire, the first debt, and which partners to bring in.

Room for the hard years. By our count of the show's feed, the typical 2026 episode runs about an hour, long enough for the slow stretches and near-misses that a tightly edited founder story leaves out.

Who Should Choose Another Show

It is a young show. The first episode aired November 18, 2025, and new ones come every few weeks, so the catalog is about 20 episodes next to shows with hundreds. If you want household-name founders, pick How I Built This or The Diary of a CEO. If you want deep company history, pick Acquired or Founders.

Overview

Two hosts research one company in depth and tell its full story, from founding through the decisions that made it. Recent subjects on the show's episode list include Home Depot and Seinfeld.

Best Fit For

Investors and operators who love business history.

Notable Strengths

For a founder, the value is in the decision points: when a company chose to raise, expand, or take on a competitor, and what each choice cost. The show's site says it reaches over one million listeners every episode and points to a Harvard Business School case written about the podcast itself, The Acquired Podcast: Scaling the Mic. In our view it is as close as podcasting gets to a good business history book.

Potential Considerations

The median 2026 episode runs about four hours, and the subjects are very large companies and franchises, such as The Home Depot (September 2026) and Seinfeld (October 2026). You hear about the founders rather than from them.

Why It Was Included

It gives founders the long view interview shows rarely have time for. Its feed opens with a trailer dated October 1, 2015, and the hosts marked ten years with a December 2025 episode featuring Michael Lewis.

Overview

David Senra reads a biography, autobiography, or memoir of an entrepreneur and walks through what he learned from it. The show's feed runs to 439 numbered episodes, the latest released November 4, 2026, and Fortune reports that Senra runs it independently, without investors or a corporate parent.

Best Fit For

Founders who learn from history.

Notable Strengths

It is a strong way to learn from builders who can no longer give interviews. Fortune reports that Senra has read 415 biographies of operators and, by his account, that listeners include Jeff Bezos, Tobi Lütke, Brian Armstrong, Michael Dell, and Daniel Ek. The same habits recur across many episodes, which makes the lessons stick.

Potential Considerations

It is one person's reading of a book, and the subjects skew toward famous, very large companies, so a founder of a small business will be translating every lesson.

Why It Was Included

It is itself a founder story worth knowing. Fortune reports that Senra is the show's only employee, that almost no one listened for its first five and a half years, and that, by his own description, it now earns millions of dollars a year in profit. The episode feed runs from a September 2016 episode on Elon Musk to number 439, on Eiichiro Oda, in November 2026.

Overview

In September 2026, TheWrap reported that Raz is acquiring full ownership of the show from NPR, and the announcement named Vox Media as partner for sales, marketing, distribution, and business operations, with video episodes on YouTube and twice as many episodes promised for early 2027. Best Fit For

Anyone curious how famous companies started.

Notable Strengths

The September 2026 announcement cites hundreds of millions of downloads, Edison Research ranked it 50th, a new entry, among U.S. podcasts in Q2 2026, and TheWrap notes Webby, Ambie, and iHeartRadio podcast awards. In our view it is well produced and good at slowing down at the moment a company nearly failed.

Potential Considerations

Guests are mostly founders of well-known consumer brands, and the edited arc can make the path look tidier than it was. Listings that still call it an NPR show are out of date.

Why It Was Included

With more than 700 episodes since 2016, it is the most familiar place for a new founder to hear how a company got its start, and the release says it will publish twice as many episodes in early 2027. For the ownership change and the shows closest to it, see our podcasts like How I Built This.

How to Choose the Right Show for You

  • You want to start a business and have not picked one: My First Million for ideas, then The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts to hear what the first years really cost. For a small, self-funded company, see our best small business podcasts, which profiles The $100 MBA Show, The Side Hustle Show, and The How of Business.
  • You own or run a private company: The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts, then Acquired for perspective on how big companies were built.
  • You want inspiration from famous founders: How I Built This.
  • You are scaling a venture-backed company: Masters of Scale.
  • You learn best from history: Founders and Acquired.
  • You want broader personal growth: The Diary of a CEO and The Tim Ferriss Show.

Choose two from different rows. A show about famous founders and a show about everyday owners, heard side by side, give a truer picture of starting a company than either one alone.

Shows We Checked and Left Off

Entrepreneurs on Fire has posted a founder interview every day for years, and its feed holds more than 4,000 episodes. In our view its short daily format trades depth for frequency, and Founders and How I Built This cover the founder story more fully, so we profile it on our podcasts like How I Built This list instead.

We also checked The GaryVee Audio Experience. Its feed describes a mix of Podcast With Friends episodes, WineText TV recaps, keynote speeches, interviews, and fireside chats from Gary Vaynerchuk, and it posted more than 200 episodes in 2026 through November 11. In our view that makes it one entrepreneur's advice on marketing and attention rather than founders explaining how a company was built, which is what this list compares.

Common questions

FAQ

What is the best entrepreneurship podcast in 2026?

The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts, the show that publishes this list, is our top pick, for listeners who want owners of privately held companies to explain how they built them. As of November 2026, the strongest of the others are How I Built This for founder origin stories, Acquired for company histories, Founders for lessons from biographies, and My First Million for business ideas.

What are the best podcasts for entrepreneurs in 2026?

Our eight picks, checked in November 2026, are The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts, Acquired, The Diary of a CEO, Founders, How I Built This, Masters of Scale, My First Million, and The Tim Ferriss Show. Every one released new episodes in 2026. Which is best depends on your stage: ideas, starting out, running a company, or scaling one.

What podcast should I listen to if I want to start a business?

Start with My First Million to find and test ideas, then add a show where owners walk through the first years, such as The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts or How I Built This. If you plan a small, self-funded company, The $100 MBA Show offers practical lessons of about 22 minutes (the 2026 median), three times a week; our list of the best small business podcasts profiles it, along with The Side Hustle Show and The How of Business.

Is How I Built This good for someone starting a business?

In our view it is better for motivation than for instructions. Its guests are mostly founders of well-known brands, and a produced story can make the early years sound smoother than they were. Pair it with My First Million for ideas and with a show where owners of ordinary private companies describe their first years, such as The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts.

How long are entrepreneurship podcast episodes?

It varies widely. Masters of Scale runs about 30 minutes and The $100 MBA Show about 22, most founder interview shows run about an hour, The Diary of a CEO usually runs 90 minutes to two hours, and the median 2026 episode of Acquired runs about four hours.

Which entrepreneurship podcasts are best for learning from famous founders?

How I Built This interviews founders of well-known brands, Founders retells the biographies of history's great builders, and Masters of Scale holds short interviews with sitting CEOs, many recorded live at its Summit.

How did you choose the shows on this list?

We looked for shows that help someone build a company, at any stage from idea to scale, and that released episodes in 2026. Fame and download counts did not decide it.
