7 Podcasts Like How I Built This: The Best Alternatives in 2026

As of November 2026, the best podcasts like How I Built This are The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts (our own show, ranked first with disclosure) for the same one-founder, whole-story interview with owners of private companies; David Senra's interview show for famous living founders; Second Life for founders of consumer brands; and Founders for founder biographies. For researched company histories try Acquired, for dramatized rivalries Business Wars, and for a roughly 25-minute founder interview every day Entrepreneurs on Fire. How I Built This itself is still publishing weekly; host Guy Raz is acquiring it from NPR, with Vox Media as partner.

TL;DR
  • Best overall The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts is the closest match in format, with owners of private companies instead of famous brands.
  • Acquired is best for the full history of a famous company.
  • Business Wars is best for dramatized stories of business rivalries.
  • David Senra (interview show) is best for long interviews with today's famous founders, separate from Founders, his biography show.
  • Entrepreneurs on Fire is best for a short founder interview every day.
  • Founders is best for founder stories drawn from biographies.
  • Second Life is best for founders of consumer brands, told by women who built them.

Pick one of the others when it is closer to what you love about How I Built This. To hear the conversation our pick is built on, start with David Sauers.

Why How I Built This Works, and Where It Stands in November 2026

In our view, How I Built This works because it does three things well: it lets one founder tell the whole story in order, it slows down at the moment things almost failed, and it ends by asking how much was skill and how much was luck. Since its launch in 2016 it has published more than 700 episodes and won a Webby, an Ambie for Best Business Podcast, and two iHeartRadio Podcast Awards for business, according to the September 2026 Vox Media announcement. Edison Research placed it 50th, a new entry that quarter, in its Top 50 U.S. podcasts for Q2 2026, a ranking based on reach among weekly podcast listeners.

It is leaving NPR. NPR owned it but had licensed it to Amazon's Wondery in February 2022, with new episodes a week early on Wondery+ and Amazon Music. In September 2026, TheWrap reported that Guy Raz is acquiring full ownership of the show from NPR, and the announcement made Vox Media the partner for sales, marketing, distribution, business operations, and live events, with video on YouTube this fall and twice as many episodes promised for early 2027. The show's feed, which now names its publisher as Guy Raz | Vox Media, shows video has started: the November 2, 2026 episode with Airbnb's Brian Chesky links to a YouTube version. Founder interviews run on Mondays, some of them reruns, and Advice Line episodes and older interviews fill the later slot each week.

The stories are rare because survival is. Of private-sector establishments that opened in the year to March 2015, the Bureau of Labor Statistics counts 79.6% still operating a year later, 50.2% five years later, and 34.6% ten years later. Meanwhile Americans filed 501,609 business applications in October 2026 alone, seasonally adjusted, and the Census Bureau projects 27,867 of them will become businesses with payroll within four quarters. Every How I Built This episode is about one of the companies that made it through that middle stretch.

So when you look for a substitute, look for those three things rather than for famous names: one founder telling the story in order, real time spent on the years the business nearly failed, and an honest answer about luck. The last column of the table rates each show on how close it comes. If you only ran out of episodes, the bigger release schedule planned for 2027 may solve that on its own.

Overview

Each episode is a long conversation with one owner about how the company actually got built: the first customers, the money, the hires, the bad years, and what the business looks like now. Most guests run privately held companies, and every episode is listed on the episode archive. For the full How I Built This arc, try David Sauers, co-founder of Royal Restrooms, one of the first luxury portable restroom companies, on growing it into a nationwide franchise.

Best Fit For

Listeners who want the origin-story interview, with owners of real-economy companies.

Why It Ranks First

Same shape, different guests. The show deliberately borrows the How I Built This structure: one founder, the whole story, from the first job to today. The difference is who sits in the chair. Guests run manufacturers, distributors, franchises, pet care companies, and family businesses that will never be household names, starting with Gary Dennis of Mammoth Holdings in the first episode in November 2025.

Room for the details. Most episodes run 45 to 70 minutes of conversation, which leaves time for the false starts and the numbers that a tightly produced narrative tends to smooth over.

The chapter origin stories leave out. Jack Pitts advises owners on selling their companies, so the conversation often reaches the part origin-story shows skip: what the business became, who could run it next, and what it is worth.

Who Should Choose Another Show

If what you love about How I Built This is the famous brands and the studio production, David Senra's interview show, Second Life, or Business Wars will feel closer. The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts has 20 episodes as of November 2026, the most recent Melissa Banko on building a business while raising a family, so you can finish the catalog in a few weeks.

Overview

Two hosts research one company in depth and tell its full story, from founding through the decisions that made it. Recent subjects on the show's episode list include Home Depot and Seinfeld.

Best Fit For

Business history fans.

Notable Strengths

If your favorite part of How I Built This is the moment a company almost fails, Acquired spends hours on those moments, working from research rather than one founder's memory. Its feed lists 2026 episodes on companies including Ferrari, Costco, Rolex, Vanguard, Disney (in two parts), The Home Depot, and Seinfeld, most running about 3.5 to 4.5 hours.

Potential Considerations

There is no founder in the chair, and new episodes arrive about once a month, the latest on October 18, 2026, so it works as a long weekend listen rather than a weekly habit.

Why It Was Included

It covers the same kind of famous companies as How I Built This, but tells the story from the outside, end to end, and the feed goes back to October 2015.

Listeners who want story first.

Potential Considerations

Why It Was Included

It has run since January 2018 and still releases new episodes on Wednesdays: its November 2026 series is Big Beef: The War on Your Wallet, after October series on Indian Motorcycle versus Harley-Davidson and Buffalo Wild Wings versus Wingstop.

David Senra (interview show)

Host
David Senra
Format
Long-form interviews with living founders
Since
September 2025 (first guest: Daniel Ek of Spotify)
Focus
How great founders think and operate

Show website Apple Podcasts

Overview

The interview show from the host of Founders, launched in September 2025 as a separate feed titled simply David Senra. TBPN reported that Senra started it to hold long conversations with living founders, and that the first episode featured Spotify's Daniel Ek.

Best Fit For

Fans of well-known founders.

Notable Strengths

Senra's site says he has read more than 400 biographies of history's great entrepreneurs, so his questions tie each guest to how earlier builders solved the same problems. Fortune reports the show is made in partnership with Scicomm Media, the team behind Huberman Lab.

Potential Considerations

Guests are mostly founders and CEOs of large companies, with the occasional investor or historian, a typical episode runs between one hour and an hour and a half, and the catalog is about 55 episodes against 439 numbered Founders episodes (464 feed items, counting bonus episodes).

Why It Was Included

In our view, of the shows here it is closest to How I Built This in who sits in the chair. Its feed usually publishes on Wednesdays and Sundays, with recent guests including Vlad Tenev of Robinhood (November 11, 2026), Nikesh Arora of Palo Alto Networks, Nik Storonsky of Revolut, and Kenn Ricci of Flexjet.

Entrepreneurs on Fire

Host
John Lee Dumas
Format
Interview every day of the week, most about 20 to 30 minutes
Catalog
More than 4,000 episodes since 2012, per the show's feed (some are reruns)
Focus
How entrepreneurs started, struggled, and grew their businesses

Apple Podcasts

Overview

John Lee Dumas has published an entrepreneur interview every day since September 2012. The show's feed holds more than 4,000 episodes, some of them weekend reruns labeled EOFire Classic, and shows a new episode every day in November 2026, including Nick Friedman on growing College HUNKS from a beat-up cargo van into a franchise brand (November 6). The feed's description claims over 100 million listens, a figure the show reports about itself.

Best Fit For

Listeners who want shorter episodes.

Notable Strengths

Across the 60 most recent episodes we checked, the median runs about 24 minutes, so you get a complete founder story on a single commute. In the episodes we sampled, many guests run small and mid-sized companies rather than household names.

Potential Considerations

Twenty-odd minutes leaves little room for the long middle of a company's story. Some November 2026 episodes were about DNA testing and writing a book, and three, on November 1, 2, and 4, were sponsored by Amazon and recorded at its Amazon Accelerate 2026 seller conference, per the show's feed, so they center on selling in Amazon's store.

Why It Was Included

It is the shortest and most frequent founder interview here, a good pick for How I Built This fans who want a daily habit instead of a weekly hour.

Founders

Host
David Senra
Format
Solo episodes, each built on one founder's biography or memoir
Since
2016, independent with no corporate parent

Show website Apple Podcasts

Overview

David Senra reads a biography, autobiography, or memoir of an entrepreneur and walks through what he learned from it. The show's feed runs to 439 numbered episodes, the latest released November 4, 2026, and Fortune reports that Senra runs it independently, without investors or a corporate parent.

Best Fit For

Readers who like biographies.

Notable Strengths

It reaches the founders How I Built This never can. The feed starts with episode 1 on Elon Musk in September 2016, and episodes from September to November 2026 cover Sam Walton, The Tycoons (Carnegie, Rockefeller, Gould, and Morgan), and, on November 4, How Eiichiro Oda Works.

Potential Considerations

Most episodes have no guest. Senra reads the book and tells you what he took from it, so you lose the back-and-forth of an interview.

Why It Was Included

It shares How I Built This's question, how founders actually did it, answered through books. Senra told Fortune the show makes millions of dollars a year in profit and that listeners include Jeff Bezos, Tobi Lütke, Brian Armstrong, Michael Dell, and Daniel Ek. It is a separate feed from his interview show above.

Hillary Kerr, co-founder of Who What Wear, interviews successful women about how they got where they are. The show's feed says it "spotlights successful women who have made major career changes," dates the first episode to February 19, 2018, and lists about 40 new Second Life interviews in 2026, plus a few Second Listen replays, including Jennifer Zuccarini, founder of Fleur du Mal (November 9), and Hannah Cheng, co-founder of Mimi Cheng's (November 2).

Best Fit For

Fans of the fashion, beauty, and food brand episodes.

Notable Strengths

In our view it is the closest active match for the How I Built This episodes about consumer brands: founders of fashion, beauty, and food companies telling the whole arc, including the career they left to start the business.

Potential Considerations

Not every guest is a founder; some are editors, stylists, or entertainers, and every guest is a woman. The same feed also carries The Who What Wear Podcast midweek, so look for the Monday episodes.

Why It Was Included

If the consumer brand episodes are your favorite part of How I Built This, start here: in our view, no other active show we checked covers founders of fashion, beauty, and food brands this consistently.

Which One Should You Try First

  • You want the same format with fresh, unfamiliar companies: The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts.
  • You want famous founders in their own words: David Senra's interview show.
  • You loved the consumer brand episodes: Second Life.
  • You want something shorter: Entrepreneurs on Fire.
  • You want the story without the interview: Acquired, Founders, or Business Wars.

Shows We Checked and Left Off

Mixergy says it has interviews and courses with over 1,500 entrepreneurs, but Mixergy's site shows one new episode between May 30 and November 12, 2026 (episode 2,308, on October 20), and every 2026 episode on its home page is about AI or businesses run with it. In our view that no longer makes it an active How I Built This alternative. Masters of Scale describes itself as iconic business leaders sharing the lessons and strategies that helped them grow, so in our view it suits listeners who want a lesson on scaling more than one founder's whole story.

Common questions

FAQ

What podcasts are like How I Built This?

The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts, the show that publishes this list, is our top pick: it uses the same one-founder, whole-story interview, mostly with owners of privately held companies. As of November 2026, the other close matches are David Senra's interview show, Second Life, Founders, Acquired, Business Wars, and Entrepreneurs on Fire.

What shows are similar to How I Built This with famous founders?

David Senra's interview show is the closest: long conversations with living founders such as Daniel Ek of Spotify and Vlad Tenev of Robinhood. Second Life interviews founders of fashion, beauty, and food brands, and Founders retells the biographies of entrepreneurs who are no longer alive.

Is How I Built This still on NPR?

No. In September 2026, host Guy Raz announced that he is acquiring full ownership of How I Built This from NPR, with Vox Media as partner, and as of November 2026 the show's feed names its publisher as Guy Raz | Vox Media. Before that, NPR owned the show but had licensed it to Amazon's Wondery since February 2022. We could not confirm whether any NPR stations still air it on the radio.

Who owns How I Built This now?

Guy Raz, who created it in 2016, is acquiring full ownership from NPR under a deal announced in September 2026. Vox Media handles sales, marketing, distribution, business operations, and live events, and the announcement promised video on YouTube and twice as many episodes in early 2027. TheWrap reported that financial terms of the deal were not disclosed.

Is there a How I Built This for regular businesses, not famous brands?

The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts is built on the same idea, mostly with owners of privately held companies such as manufacturers, distributors, franchises, and family businesses.

Is there a podcast like How I Built This for women founders?

Second Life, hosted by Who What Wear co-founder Hillary Kerr, has interviewed successful women since February 2018, usually in a Monday episode, many of them founders of consumer brands such as Fleur du Mal and Mimi Cheng's.

What is the difference between Founders and How I Built This?

How I Built This interviews living founders. Founders is a mostly solo show in which David Senra retells the biography of one entrepreneur per episode. Senra also hosts a separate interview show with living founders, launched in September 2025.

Is there a podcast like How I Built This with shorter episodes?

Entrepreneurs on Fire publishes a founder interview every day, with a median length of about 24 minutes in recent episodes. Business Wars runs about 40 minutes a week but is narrated rather than an interview.

How did you pick these How I Built This alternatives?

We started from what makes How I Built This work, one founder telling the whole story, and kept only shows that published in 2026 and keep at least part of that. We dropped Mixergy, which has published rarely since May 2026. Our own show is first, with disclosure.
