The Best Podcasts About Buying and Selling a Business in 2026

The best podcasts about buying and selling a business in 2026 are The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts (our own show, ranked first with disclosure) for how owners built companies worth buying, Built to Sell Radio for the sale itself, Acquiring Minds for buying a small business, and M&A Science for corporate deal process. The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts is hosted by the co-founder of a sell-side M&A firm and, in our view, is the best choice if you want the whole story of how an owner built a company worth buying.

Podcast Listening in 2026

Podcast listening is at a record high. The Infinite Dial 2026, released by Edison Research at SSRS with support from SiriusXM Media, found that 58% of Americans age 12 and older, about 167 million people, consumed a podcast in the last month, an all-time high. Forty-five percent, about 130 million people, did so in the last week, and 57% have both listened to and watched a podcast.

Business owners are well represented in that audience. National Public Media, citing Edison Research's Infinite Dial 2025, reports that 18% of monthly podcast listeners own a business, that 47% earn $75,000 or more a year, and that 51% have a college degree.

With that many shows competing for the same hour, the useful question is not which show is biggest. It is which one is built for a listener like you.

What the 2026 Deal Market Means for Buyers and Sellers

Small business deals are still getting done, but buyers are choosier. BizBuySell's Q3 2026 Insight Report counted 2,132 businesses sold in the third quarter, flat from the second quarter and down 7% from a year earlier, while the median sale price rose 17% to $350,000. BizBuySell president Bob House put it simply: buyers have not left the market, they are becoming more selective.

Selling also takes longer than most owners expect. In the IBBA and M&A Source Market Pulse for Q2 2026, Main Street deals averaged six to 10 months from engagement to close and lower middle market deals 11 to 12 months, while 87% of deals over $5 million drew at least three offers.

And the reason owners sell is often not retirement. Built to Sell discussed a review of 10,255 of its assessments over six years in which 17.5% of owners pointed to burnout, which the episode calls the leading reason owners want out, ahead of retirement. A good podcast on this topic helps on both counts: it shows buyers what a well-run company looks like, and it helps owners build one before they are tired of running it.

Overview

Each episode is a long conversation with one owner about how the company actually got built: the first customers, the money, the hires, the bad years, and what the business looks like now. Most guests run privately held companies, and every episode is listed on the episode archive. Start with Dave Moorman, who grew a managed IT company through acquisitions and exited twice.

Best Fit For

Owners who may sell one day, and buyers who want to know what they are buying.

Why It Ranks First

The host works on sell-side deals for a living. Jack Pitts co-founded Salt Creek Advisory, a sell-side M&A firm, so the questions go where buyers will look: margins, customer concentration, key people, debt, and who could run the company without the founder.

You hear the build, not only the exit. What a buyer pays for is decided over years. Episodes such as Dave Moorman on building an MSP, growing through acquisitions, and exiting twice, and Gary Dennis on building Mammoth Holdings into a leading car wash platform, follow the whole path.

Owners of companies you rarely hear from. Guests run family businesses, distributors, manufacturers, and service companies: the kind of privately held companies that small and lower middle market buyers actually acquire.

Who Should Choose Another Show

Be clear on what this show is. It is a general owner-story show, and only some episodes focus on acquisitions and exits. It launched in November 2025, so the catalog is small. If you want a deal topic every week, pick Built to Sell Radio for the sell side or Acquiring Minds for the buy side.

Overview

Interviews with people who bought an existing small business, covering how they found it, financed it, and ran it after closing. The official site calls it a media platform about buying, owning, and operating small businesses, cites a community of 4,778 acquisition entrepreneurs, and lists recent episodes such as “She Wanted to Run a Factory, So She Bought One” (November 9, 2026) and “Run It for 6 Months Before You Buy It” (October 29, 2026).

Best Fit For

Searchers and first-time small business buyers.

Notable Strengths

Detailed deal mechanics, such as SBA loans, seller notes, partnerships, and diligence surprises, told by the buyers who did the deals.

Potential Considerations

It is told from the buyer's side. Owners planning to sell will learn how buyers think, which is valuable, but it is not sell-side advice.

Why It Was Included

It is a focused interview show on buying an existing small business, and in our view a strong starting point for first-time buyers.

Acquisitions Anonymous

Hosts
Michael Girdley, Mills Snell, Bill D'Alessandro, and Heather Endresen
Format
Panel breakdowns of real businesses for sale, about 30 to 45 minutes, twice a week
Focus
Buying, selling, and operating small businesses

Show website Apple Podcasts

Overview

Four hosts take a real business listing and debate whether they would buy it and at what price. The show's site introduces the panel: Girdley co-founded Dura Software, Snell is COO of Aqua Seal Roofing and previously ran a sell-side M&A advisory firm, D'Alessandro founded Elements Brands, and Endresen has more than 30 years in banking and leads Viso Business Capital. Its Apple Podcasts listing shows recent episodes on a mobile RV repair business said to make $105,000 a year and a $23 million Caribbean construction company.

Best Fit For

Buyers, and owners curious how buyers judge them.

Notable Strengths

You hear how experienced buyers, an operator, and a lender read the same listing: what they trust, what worries them, and what drives price. Useful for owners who will one day sell.

Potential Considerations

The hosts are reacting to public listings, so the analysis is quick and informal rather than a full valuation.

Why It Was Included

It walks through real listings from the buyer's chair twice a week, which makes it useful on both sides of a deal.

The show's page lists episode 571, published November 6, 2026, in which a distributor sold at 9.5 times EBITDA after private equity had offered 5 times. Best Fit For

Owners preparing for a sale.

Notable Strengths

In our view, no show on this list stays closer to the owner's side of a sale. Episodes get into multiples, earn-outs, rollover equity, and the different ways private equity, strategic buyers, and searchers price a company, often with real numbers.

Potential Considerations

Nearly every episode is about the exit. If you are years from selling, pair it with a show about running and growing the company in the meantime.

Why It Was Included

It is a long-running podcast devoted to owners selling their companies, which makes it the natural pick for the sell side.

How to Choose the Right Show for Your Side of the Deal

  • You own a company and might sell in the next few years: Built to Sell Radio and ExitReadiness Podcast, plus The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts for how other owners built something worth buying.
  • You want to buy your first small business: Acquiring Minds, then Acquisitions Anonymous to practice reading listings.
  • You are raising or running a search fund: Search Funded: The ETA Podcast.
  • You work in corporate development: M&A Science.
  • You want to grow by acquisition while you own the company: The Making Of Hosted By Jack Pitts and Acquiring Minds.

Listen to one show from each side. Sellers learn the most from hearing how buyers think, and buyers learn the most from hearing what owners went through to build the company.
